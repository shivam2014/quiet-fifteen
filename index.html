01The cheque

Seven premiums, one cheque,
6.96% a year.

Seven annual premiums of ₹1,22,067 went out between 2011 and 2017. One cheque for ₹19,34,520 came back on 6 November 2026.

The gain is ₹10,80,054, or 2.26 times the money paid in. The multiple is easy to read but misleading on its own, because the premiums went out over seven years. Measured across the actual dates, the policy compounded at 6.96% a year. It was issued before April 2012, and the base annual premium of ₹1.20L was 14.3% of the ₹8.40L sum assured. That is inside the 20% limit, so the maturity is tax-free under Section 10(10D).

At a 30% tax slab, a tax-free 6.96% is equivalent to a 9.94% taxable return. That is the right yardstick for the bank comparison: the policy beat the fixed deposit after tax. It roughly matched a 10% bond and did not beat equity or gold.

04The tax lens

A tax-free 6.96%
is not a taxable 6.96%.

Tax decides how much of the return you keep. The policy was an EEE product: exempt on the way in, on the way up, and on the way out.

At a 30% slab, the fixed deposit's 6.74% return becomes 4.71% after tax. A 10% bond becomes 7.00%, a hair above the policy. At 35%, the bond falls to 6.50% and the policy is ahead. The policy beats the fixed deposit at every slab. Its 6.96% is already net, equivalent to 9.94% taxable.

05The real return

Inflation took most
of the nominal gain.

Between 2011 and 2026, India's consumer price index rose about 2.26 times, or 5.58% a year.

After inflation, the policy returned 1.77% a year. It preserved purchasing power and added a little. The fixed deposit lost 0.36% a year in real terms, and the 10% bond returned 1.81%. Equity and gold compounded real wealth, but only for investors who could tolerate the drawdowns. The policy did the job it was bought to do: it protected capital.

06The price of certainty

The extra return came
with a drawdown.

Volatility is measured from monthly returns between 2011 and 2026.

The policy had no volatility, no sequence risk, ₹8.40L of life cover, and a cheque that could not shrink. Equity and gold paid more because they asked more: 16% to 17% annual volatility, a 38% drawdown for the Nifty in 2020, and a 25% drawdown for gold in 2013-15. If you had needed the money in March 2020, the comparison would have looked different.

07The verdict

A guaranteed 6.96%.
An ₹11.84 lakh gap.

The policy kept its promise. Whether that was the right promise depends on what you needed the money to do.

What it did well

It beat the bank.

At a 30% slab, the fixed deposit ended ₹4.43L behind. It roughly matched a 10% bond; at a 35% slab it beat the bond. It delivered 6.96% with no volatility, added life cover, and required one decision a year.

  • Tax-free under 10(10D)
  • No market or sequence risk
  • ₹8.40L life cover
  • Automatic, annual saving
What it cost

The opportunity cost.

The Nifty ended ₹11.84L ahead after tax, and gold ended ₹16.81L ahead. The real return was 1.77% a year, and the money was locked up for fifteen years. There was no explicit fee. The cost was the lower return.

  • 1.77% real annual return
  • Capital locked for 15 years
  • No participation in India's equity compounding
  • Inflation absorbed most of the nominal return
What I would do with the cheque

Split it by purpose.

The ₹19.35L is now a liquid, tax-free lump sum. The useful question is what each part of it is for. Money you cannot afford to lose belongs in a guaranteed bucket. Money you can leave alone for a decade belongs in a diversified portfolio.

  • 3 to 5 years of spending in the guaranteed bucket
  • The rest in a diversified, low-cost portfolio
  • Do not chase gold after a fivefold rise
  • Remember that reinvesting the maturity starts a new tax clock
The policy returned 6.96% a year and never fell. The Nifty returned 11.18% after tax and fell 38% in 2020.
